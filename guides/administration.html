Administration Guide

Grade 7 ELA Pre/Post Assessment · Form A (Pre-Test) and Form B (Post-Test)

1. Before you start — the one rule that matters most

Whatever conditions you use in September, use again in April. Same time allocation, same accommodations, same room, same time of day, same read-aloud decisions, same scorers for the essay. A pre/post comparison is only interpretable if the only thing that changed between the two administrations is the instruction.

If a condition has to change — a student moves to a different section, a new teacher scores the essays — record it in the Accommodations column of the workbook so that student's growth is interpreted with that in mind.

2. When to administer

Administration Window Why
Form A (Pre-Test) Within the first three weeks of school Late enough that students have settled, early enough that the data can still shape the year. Administering in week one measures summer, not September.
Form B (Post-Test) Three to four weeks before the Georgia Milestones window Late enough to capture the year's instruction, early enough that the results can still drive a final review cycle. Do not administer it the week before the Milestones — students are saturated and the data arrives too late to use.

Do not review Form A items with students. Return the results, discuss categories and standards, and keep the item content secure. Reviewing Form A contaminates Form B growth. The Student Report is built to support that conversation without exposing items.

3. Materials

Per student: - Form A or Form B booklet (print single-sided or double-sided; do not staple the passages separately from the items — students need to flip back) - Three pages of lined paper for the extended writing task - Pencil, and a highlighter if your students are used to annotating

Per teacher: - Answer key for the form being administered (secure) - rubrics/Writing-Rubric-and-Scoring-Guide.md - The reporting workbook

Not permitted: dictionaries, thesauruses, spell-check, internet access, phones. Item 24 and item 37 assess citation and bibliographic reading using the Works Cited list printed in the booklet — no external lookup is needed or allowed.

4. Timing

Preferred: two 60-minute sessions on consecutive days.

Session Parts Points Time
Session 1 Part 1 (Literary & Poetic) + Part 2 (Paired Informational) 34 55 min + 5 min directions
Session 2 Part 3 (Peer Revision Task) + Part 4 (Extended Writing) 26 55 min + 5 min directions

Alternative: one 120-minute block with a 5-minute stretch break between Part 2 and Part 3. Use this only if your schedule forces it — fatigue on Part 4 will depress Trait scores, and if it depresses them at pre-test only, it will manufacture false growth.

These are working times, not hard limits. This is a diagnostic instrument, not a speed test. Students who need more time should get it, as long as they get the same additional time at both administrations. Record extended time in the Accommodations column.

5. Administration script

Read aloud. Do not paraphrase the parts in quotation marks — students should hear the same framing at both administrations.

Session 1

"Today you are taking a Grade 7 English Language Arts assessment. This does not count as a test grade. Its purpose is to show me exactly which reading and writing skills you already have and which ones we need to work on together, so that I can plan lessons that actually fit this class.

You will read a short story, a poem, a summary of a myth, and two articles about the same topic. Then you will answer questions about them.

Three things to know. First, you may write in this booklet — underline, take notes, cross out answers you have ruled out. Second, you may go back to the passages at any time; you are not supposed to remember everything. Third, some questions are worth two points. Those have a Part A and a Part B, or they ask you to select TWO answers. Read the directions on every question.

If you are not sure of an answer, choose the one you think is most likely and move on. A blank earns nothing; a good guess sometimes earns a point. You have 55 minutes. Begin."

At 10 minutes remaining: "Ten minutes left. If you are still in Part 1, move to Part 2 now and come back."

Session 2

"Today you finish the assessment. Part 3 is a Peer Revision Task — you will read a draft written by another student and answer questions about how to make it better. This is the same kind of task that appears on the Georgia Milestones.

Part 4 asks you to write an argumentative essay using the two sources you read yesterday. Those sources are printed in your booklet, and you are expected to go back to them. Your essay will be scored on three things: how you organize your argument, how you use and explain evidence from both sources, and your sentences and conventions.

There is a planning box before the lined pages. Use it — planning is not scored, but it shows in the writing. You have 55 minutes for both parts. I recommend about 25 minutes on Part 3 and 30 on your essay. Begin."

At 20 minutes remaining: "Twenty minutes left. If you have not started writing your essay, start now." At 5 minutes remaining: "Five minutes. Make sure you have a conclusion."

What you may and may not clarify

A student asks… You may say
"What does this word mean?" (in a direction) Explain the direction freely.
"What does this word mean?" (in a passage or item) "That's part of what the question is asking. Use what's around it."
"Do I have to use both sources?" "Yes. Read the bullet list in the prompt again."
"Can I disagree with the article?" "Yes. You can argue either side, as long as you support it with evidence from the sources."
"Is this long enough?" "Long enough to make your argument and support it with evidence from both sources."
"Which answer is right?" Nothing. Redirect to the text.

6. Accommodations

Follow each student's IEP, 504, or EL plan. Apply identical accommodations at pre-test and post-test. Record each student's accommodations in the workbook's Roster sheet.

Accommodation Effect on score interpretation
Extended time None, provided it is given at both administrations
Small group / separate setting None
Frequent breaks None
Scribe for the essay Trait 3 (conventions) is compromised — the scribe's conventions, not the student's. Note it and interpret Trait 3 cautiously.
Read-aloud of Part 2 sources during Part 3 and Part 4 None. Constructing Texts is a writing measure; source access is intended.
Read-aloud of Part 1 or Part 2 during Session 1 Compromises the Interpreting Texts reading measure. Still valuable diagnostically — it separates decoding from comprehension — but flag it in Accommodations and do not compare that student's Interpreting Texts score to unaccommodated peers.
Translated directions / bilingual dictionary (EL) Directions: no effect. A dictionary invalidates items 3, 14, 15, 19, and 36 (vocabulary). Flag it.

7. Short administration (52 points, no essay)

If you need a pure Milestones-format dry run with no extended writing:

  1. Administer Parts 1–3 only (items 1–38). One 90-minute session or two 45-minute sessions.
  2. Leave Q39_T1, Q39_T2, and Q39_T3 blank in the reporting workbook.
  3. On the Settings sheet, change Total points possible (B8) to 52 and adjust the three cut scores proportionally: Developing 26, Proficient 34, Distinguished 43.

Constructing Texts then reports on 18 points instead of 26, and category weighting shifts away from the blueprint. Say so when you report the results. The full form is the defensible one; use the short form for a mid-year check, not for your pre/post pair.

8. Scoring workflow

Budget roughly 90 minutes per class of 25 for the whole cycle.

Step 1 — Score items 1–38 (about 25 minutes per class). Use the answer key. Apply the multi-point rules exactly:

  • EBSR / multiple-part: 2 = both parts; 1 = one part; 0 = neither.
  • Multiple-select ("Select TWO"): 2 = both correct with no incorrect marks; 1 = one correct with at most one incorrect; 0 if the student marked three or more.
  • Match (item 22): 2 = all four; 1 = exactly three; 0 = two or fewer.

Step 2 — Calibrate essay scoring (15 minutes, once per administration). Follow the five-paper protocol in rubrics/Writing-Rubric-and-Scoring-Guide.md §5 before scoring any student papers. Skipping this is the single most common cause of uninterpretable writing data.

Step 3 — Score the essays (about 4 minutes each). Read once without a pen, then score Trait 1, Trait 2, Trait 3 in that order. Double-score 20% of papers.

Step 4 — Enter item-level scores in the workbook's Pre-Test Entry or Post-Test Entry sheet. Enter 0 for a wrong answer, never a blank — a blank means "not tested" and removes the student from the statistics.

Step 5 — Read the reports. Student Report for individuals, Class Dashboard for the class, Intervention Groups for grouping, Item Analysis for whether the test itself behaved.

9. Using the results in the first week

Day 1 — Class Dashboard. Look at the reporting category table, not the totals. Any category where 40% or more of students are "Not Yet" is a whole-class instruction problem, and the workbook says so in the last column. Start there — it is the highest-leverage move available.

Day 2 — Intervention Groups. Sort by column F (primary group). You will typically get three to five groups. Do not run more than three at once.

Day 3 — Students flagged "NEAR CUT." These students' level labels are unreliable. Pull their actual booklets and read what they wrote. Fifteen minutes here prevents a semester of misplacement.

Day 4 — Item Analysis. Any item flagged "LOW DISCRIMINATION" or with a p-value under 0.25 should be examined before you conclude anything about the standard it measures. Sometimes the item is the problem, not the students.

Day 5 — Student conferences. Show each student their own category profile. The single most useful sentence in a Grade 7 data conference is: "Here is the one thing that will move your score the most, and here is what we're going to do about it."

10. What to say when someone over-reads the data

You will be asked to predict Milestones performance from this. Three honest sentences:

  • "These cut scores were set by content review, not by a state standard-setting panel." The levels are instructional triage, not a Milestones prediction.
  • "A category score can rest on as few as six points." Category profiles form groups; they do not diagnose individuals on their own.
  • "A gain smaller than the reliable-change threshold may be measurement noise." The workbook prints that threshold. Report small individual gains as class-level movement.

Saying these plainly protects the instrument. Data that gets over-claimed once gets ignored afterward.
