# Grade 7 ELA Pre/Post Assessment — Design & Technical Notes

**Forms:** Form A (Pre-Test) · Form B (Post-Test)
**Total points:** 60 per form
**Source of truth:** Georgia's K–12 ELA Standards, Grade 7 (Approved May 2023); Georgia Milestones Grade 7 ELA Assessment Blueprint (August 2025); Draft Grade 7 ELA Achievement Level Descriptors (October 2025, effective 2025–2026); Georgia Milestones Grade 7 Eight-Point, Three-Trait Writing Rubric (August 2026); Grade 7 Classroom Peer Revision Guidance (February 2026).

---

## 1. Purpose and intended use

This is a **criterion-referenced, curriculum-embedded pre/post instrument** built to mirror the Georgia Milestones Grade 7 ELA blueprint. Its job is not to predict a scale score. Its job is to tell a teacher, in September and again in April, *which standards each student can and cannot do yet*, at a grain size fine enough to plan Tier 1 instruction and Tier 2/3 intervention.

Three design commitments follow from that purpose:

1. **Blueprint fidelity.** Every reporting category carries the same number of points as the operational Georgia Milestones blueprint. A student's category percentages here are directly interpretable against the same weighting the state uses.
2. **Strict form parallelism.** Form B is not "another test." Every item on Form B is an isomorph of the matching Form A item — same standard, same expectation, same Depth of Knowledge, same item type, same point value, same cognitive demand — differing only in passage content and answer position. This is what makes a raw pre→post difference interpretable as growth rather than as a difference in test difficulty.
3. **Actionability.** The reporting workbook converts item scores into standard-level mastery flags and pre-built intervention groupings. Data that has to be hand-tabulated does not get used.

**What this instrument is not.** It is not equated to the Georgia Milestones scale, does not produce a Lexile measure or a Reading Status, and its achievement-level cut points are *provisional classroom cuts* set by rational (content-based) standard setting, not by a state standard-setting panel. Treat level placement as instructional triage, not as a prediction of the student's spring Milestones level. See §6.

## 2. Blueprint replication

The Georgia Milestones Grade 7 ELA Blueprint distributes 60 points across two claims. This instrument reproduces that distribution exactly.

| Claim / Reporting category | Content expectations assessed | GM points | This instrument |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Interpreting Texts** (Reading/Listening/Viewing) | | **34** | **34** |
| ├ Texts: Context + Structure & Style | 7.T.C.1.a–b, 7.T.C.2.a–c, 7.T.SS.1.a, 7.T.SS.2.a | 6 | 6 |
| ├ Texts: Techniques | 7.T.T.1.a–d, 7.T.T.2.a–b, 7.T.T.3.a, 7.T.T.4.a | 16 | 16 |
| ├ Texts: Periods & Movements + Research & Analysis | 7.T.PM.1.a–b, 7.T.RA.1.a–b, 7.T.RA.2.a–b | 6 | 6 |
| └ Language: Grammar Conventions + Vocabulary | 7.L.GC.2.a, 7.L.V.1.a, 7.L.V.2.a–b, 7.L.V.3.b–d | 6 | 6 |
| **Constructing Texts** (Writing/Speaking/Creating) | | **26** | **26** |
| ├ Texts: Context + Structure & Style | 7.T.C.1.c, 7.T.C.2.d, 7.T.SS.1.b–d, 7.T.SS.2.b–c | 8 | 8 |
| ├ Texts: Techniques | 7.T.T.1.e, 7.T.T.2.d, 7.T.T.3.c, 7.T.T.4.b | 10 | 10 |
| └ Language: Grammar Conventions + Vocabulary | 7.L.GC.1 (GUM), 7.L.GC.2.b–d, 7.L.V.1.b, 7.L.V.2.c–d, 7.L.V.3.e | 8 | 8 |
| **Total** | | **60** | **60** |

The Practices domain (K–12.P.*) is, per the blueprint, embedded within items aligned to Texts and Language standards and is **not reported as a separate category**. The item spec matrix in `02-Blueprint-Alignment-Matrix.md` records the embedded Practices standard for each item so that teachers can see, for example, that item 22 is doing the work of `K–12.P.AC.1.a` even though it reports under Techniques.

### Where this instrument deliberately departs from the operational blueprint

The operational Milestones assesses Constructing Texts entirely through 1- and 2-point selected-response and technology-enhanced items — including the **Peer Revision Task** — and does **not** include an extended constructed response. This instrument keeps the Peer Revision Task but **converts 8 of the 26 Constructing Texts points into an extended writing task scored on the Georgia Milestones Grade 7 Eight-Point, Three-Trait Writing Rubric.**

That is an intentional trade, made for three reasons:

- The rubric provided as a source is explicitly marked **"INTENDED FOR CLASSROOM USE."** It exists to be used on classroom writing.
- A pre/post instrument that never asks a student to write cannot measure the thing 7.T.SS.1.d, 7.T.T.3.c, and 7.L.GC.2.b–d actually describe: *sustained* composition. Selected-response revision items measure recognition of good writing; they do not measure production of it.
- The rubric's three traits map cleanly onto the three Constructing Texts reporting categories, so the substitution preserves the blueprint weighting rather than distorting it:

| Rubric trait | Points | Reports to Constructing Texts category |
|---|---:|---|
| Trait 1 — Purpose & Organization | 3 | Context + Structure & Style |
| Trait 2 — Evidence & Elaboration | 3 | Techniques |
| Trait 3 — Language Usage & Conventions | 2 | Language: GC + Vocabulary |

Constructing Texts therefore totals: Context/Structure & Style = 5 item points + 3 essay = **8**; Techniques = 7 item points + 3 essay = **10**; Language = 6 item points + 2 essay = **8**. Blueprint weighting is preserved to the point.

If a school wants a pure Milestones-format dry run with no essay, `03-Administration-Guide.md` §7 describes a 52-point short administration; the reporting workbook handles it via a single toggle.

## 3. Item design

### 3.1 Composition

| Item type | Items | Points |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1-point selected-response / technology-enhanced | 24 | 24 |
| 2-point technology-enhanced (multi-part, multi-select, evidence-based) | 14 | 28 |
| Extended writing task (3-trait rubric) | 1 | 8 |
| **Total** | **39** | **60** |

The operational blueprint specifies 30 one-point and 15 two-point scored items. This instrument runs 24/14 plus the essay because the essay absorbs 8 points. The *ratio* of two-point to one-point items (0.58 here, 0.50 operational) is slightly richer in multi-part items, which is deliberate: two-point evidence-based items yield partial-credit information that is far more diagnostically useful than an equal number of one-point items.

### 3.2 Technology-enhanced formats, on paper

The Milestones item types — drag-and-drop, drop-down, evidence-based selected-response, match interaction, reorder interaction, hot-text highlight, multiple-part, multiple-select — are all reproducible in a paper booklet. Each form uses paper analogues, labeled so students learn the online vocabulary:

| Milestones type | Paper analogue used here |
|---|---|
| Evidence-based selected-response (EBSR) | Part A / Part B, both required for full credit |
| Multiple-select | "Select **TWO**" with checkbox list |
| Hot-text highlight | Numbered sentences; student writes the sentence numbers |
| Drag-and-drop / match | Letter-to-box matching table |
| Reorder | Number the items 1–4 |
| Drop-down | Choose one word/phrase per numbered blank |
| Multiple-part | Part A / Part B on different skills |

### 3.3 Depth of Knowledge

Blueprint targets and instrument actuals:

| DOK | Blueprint points | Blueprint % | Form A/B points | Form A/B % | In range |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|:---:|
| Level 1 | 2–6 | 3%–10% | 3 | 5.0% | ✓ |
| Level 2 | 27–36 | 45%–60% | 29 | 48.3% | ✓ |
| Level 3 | 21–30 | 35%–50% | 28 | 46.7% | ✓ |
| Level 4 | N/A | N/A | 0 | 0% | ✓ |

The extended writing task is counted as DOK 3 in this tally. Level 1 is intentionally near the floor of the permitted band: recall-level items generate almost no instructional information, and their only real function is to keep the lowest-performing students engaged early in each section. Both Level 1 items in each section are placed among the first three items of their part.

### 3.4 Achievement Level Descriptor calibration

Item difficulty is not left to chance. Each item was written *to a specific ALD row*, so that the item's demand matches a named achievement level. This is what makes the category percentages meaningful.

| Target ALD level | Points per form | Typical item demand |
|---|---:|---|
| Beginning | 9 | *identify / recognize / determine* a single, explicitly stated element |
| Developing | 18 | *explain / describe / determine* within a **section** of text |
| Proficient | 25 | *analyze* **across a whole text**; compare two texts; apply across two paragraphs |
| Distinguished | 8 | *analyze across two texts*; support analysis with cited textual evidence |

Worked example — 7.T.T.2 (Expository Techniques), showing how one standard is sampled at three levels within a single form:

- Item 12 (1 pt, DOK 2) — *"Determine the main idea of the section…"* → **Developing** ALD: "Determine the main idea(s) or key detail(s) in an expository text that is presented using a specific technique."
- Item 13 (1 pt, DOK 2) — *"Which statistic from the table best supports…"* → **Developing/Proficient** ALD: "Determine specific facts, statistics, key details, and/or information from text features … that support the main idea(s)."
- Item 22 (2 pts, DOK 3) — *"Analyze how the two authors shape their presentations by emphasizing different evidence"* → **Proficient** ALD: "Analyze how two or more authors writing about the same topic shape their presentations of key information by emphasizing different evidence or advancing different interpretations of facts."

A student who answers 12 and 13 but misses 22 is a Developing learner on Expository Techniques, and the workbook says exactly that. That is a different instructional problem from a student who misses all three, and it gets a different intervention.

## 4. Passage design

### 4.1 Passage set architecture (identical across forms)

| Set | Texts | Length (Form A / Form B) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Set 1 — Literary & Poetic** | Modern short story that adapts a classical myth; a poem on the same myth; a boxed summary of the source myth | story 966 / 1,003 words · poem 20 lines each · myth box 91 / 85 words | Narrative techniques (7.T.T.1), poetic techniques (7.T.T.4), craft (7.T.SS.2), **Periods & Movements (7.T.PM.1.a)**, fiction-vs-account comparison (7.T.T.1.d) |
| **Set 2 — Paired Informational** | An expository article with subheadings and a data table; an editorial on the same topic with claim, counterclaim, and evidence; Works Cited entries for both | article 576 / 587 words · editorial 471 / 429 words | Expository (7.T.T.2) and argumentative (7.T.T.3) techniques, purpose/audience (7.T.C.1), perspective & credibility (7.T.C.2), organization (7.T.SS.1.a), **two-author comparison (7.T.T.2.b)**, research & curation (7.T.RA.2) |
| **Set 3 — Peer Revision Task** | A one-page student draft on the Set 2 topic, **24 numbered sentences in 5 paragraphs on both forms**, with planted, standards-specific flaws | 338 / 367 words | All Constructing Texts categories |
| **Set 4 — Extended Writing Task** | Argumentative prompt requiring both Set 2 sources | — | 3-trait rubric |

Total student reading load: **1,439 words on Form A and 1,504 on Form B** across Sets 1 and 2, plus the PRT draft. The largest single gap between paired passages is the editorial (42 words, 9%); every other pair matches within 4%.

Anchoring Sets 2, 3, and 4 to a single topic is not laziness — it is the Milestones design. It lets the Peer Revision Task and the essay be scored for *use of the sources*, which is what Trait 2 of the rubric actually demands ("evidence from multiple sources," "makes a consistent attempt to credit sources").

The myth-adaptation choice is likewise deliberate: **7.T.PM.1.a is the only Grade 7 Periods & Movements expectation that can be assessed in a single sitting** ("analyze myths and stories that modern writers have adapted into their own works"). Pairing the modern story with a boxed myth summary lets one passage set carry both 7.T.PM.1.a and 7.T.T.1.d (comparing a fictional portrayal with an account of the same period) without adding a fourth reading load.

### 4.2 Text complexity control

Grade 7 stretch band is Lexile 925L–1185L. Passages were written to hold quantitative and qualitative complexity constant across forms:

| Passage | Form A | Form B | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short story | *Wax Wings* (Icarus) — 966 words, 28 ¶ | *Thread Count* (Arachne) — 1,003 words, 28 ¶ | 950L–1050L |
| Poem | *Flight Lessons* — 4 stanzas, 20 lines (6/6/7/1) | *The Loom* — 4 stanzas, 20 lines (6/6/7/1) | n/a (qualitative) |
| Expository article | *The Vanishing Night* (light pollution) — 576 words | *The Mountain in the Drawer* (e-waste) — 587 words | 1000L–1100L |
| Editorial | *Turn Down the Lights, Merrick* — 471 words, 8 ¶ | *Let Us Fix It* — 429 words, 9 ¶ | 980L–1080L |
| Student draft (PRT) | 338 words, 24 numbered sentences, 5 ¶ | 367 words, 24 numbered sentences, 5 ¶ | below band by design |

**Structural controls applied to both forms equally.** The short stories share a paragraph-for-paragraph architecture: the flashback falls at ¶6 on both forms, the mood-setting figure of speech at ¶7, the warning dialogue at ¶8–11, the failure at ¶18–19, and the mentor's reframing at ¶27. Each expository article carries exactly one data table, three subheadings, and four statistics. Each editorial states one claim, acknowledges exactly one counterclaim, and supplies two distinct pieces of external evidence — one usable for each of the two reasons the PRT draft develops. Each PRT draft contains exactly the same *number, type, and sentence position* of planted flaws (see `02-Blueprint-Alignment-Matrix.md` §4).

**Note on the Lexile targets.** The bands above are the *design intent* for each passage slot. These are original passages that have not been submitted for official Lexile analysis, so the values are targets, not measured results. Verify them locally if your district requires reported text complexity.

Topics were chosen to be civically substantive but low in emotional load and free of prior-knowledge advantage: light pollution (A) and electronic waste (B) are both environmental-policy topics with a local-government angle, neither of which is typically pre-taught in Grade 7 science or social studies in Georgia.

### 4.3 Bias and sensitivity

All passages are original. Screening applied: no content advantaging students by family income, rural/urban residence, technology access at home, religion, athletic participation, or travel experience; no depiction of family structure required for comprehension; character names varied in cultural origin across both forms; no reference to holidays, testing, or grade-level social hierarchies. The Icarus and Arachne source myths are given in full in the boxed summary, so no student is advantaged by prior exposure to Greek mythology — the summary *is* the account being compared against.

## 5. Parallel-form equivalence

Form B is built as a **content-matched isomorph** of Form A. For each item pair, the following are held identical:

- reporting category and point value
- standard and lettered expectation
- target ALD level
- Depth of Knowledge
- item type and, for multi-part items, the structure of each part
- number of options, and the syntactic form of the stem
- for 2-point items, the partial-credit rule

The following are deliberately varied:

- passage content and topic
- position of the correct answer (key positions balanced independently on each form: across the 24 single-select items, A/B/C/D each key 6 times)
- for multi-select items, which two of the four/five options are correct
- surface features of the planted PRT errors

**Evidence of equivalence available before use:** content matching and blueprint replication (above). **Evidence not yet available:** empirical equivalence. Until you have collected data, the honest statement to make to an administrator is *"the forms are content-equivalent by construction; statistical equivalence has not been established."*

**How to establish it locally.** With one year of data (n ≥ 60 across sections), run the checks the reporting workbook computes for you on the `Item Analysis` sheet:

1. **Mean difference.** Compare the Form A pre-test mean to the Form B *pre*-test mean if you counterbalance (see below). A difference under 2 raw points (≈0.15 SD) supports equivalence.
2. **Item p-value correspondence.** Plot Form A item p-values against their Form B partners. Pairs differing by more than 0.20 are flagged automatically; those items should be revised or rotated out of the growth calculation.
3. **Internal consistency.** The workbook reports Cronbach's α for each form. Expect α ≈ 0.82–0.88 for a 39-item mixed-format instrument. If α < 0.70 on either form, category-level reporting is not yet trustworthy and should be used only as a class-level signal.

**Counterbalancing (recommended if you have four or more sections).** Assign half of your sections Form A → Form B and half Form B → Form A. This removes form effects from the growth estimate entirely and gives you the data to check equivalence directly. The reporting workbook has a per-student `Form Order` column and handles either order without any change to the formulas.

## 6. Achievement levels and cut scores

Cut points were set by a **rational (content-based) method**: for each reporting category, the points attached to items written at each target ALD level were summed, and the cut for a level was placed at the score a student would earn by mastering the items at and below that level, with a small allowance for guessing on selected-response items.

| Achievement level | Raw score (of 60) | Percent | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beginning Learner** | 0–29 | 0%–48% | Minimal command of grade-level standards. Needs substantial academic support. |
| **Developing Learner** | 30–38 | 50%–63% | Partial command. Needs additional academic support. |
| **Proficient Learner** | 39–48 | 65%–80% | Proficiency. Prepared for Grade 8. |
| **Distinguished Learner** | 49–60 | 82%–100% | Advanced proficiency. Well prepared for Grade 8. |

**These cuts are editable.** They live in named cells on the `Settings` sheet of the reporting workbook, and every level formula reads from those cells. After your first full year, replace them with locally derived cuts: rank your students' pre-test scores against their actual spring Milestones levels and move the cuts to maximize classification agreement. That single step converts this from a rationally-set instrument to a locally-validated one.

**Standard error of measurement.** For a 60-point instrument with α ≈ 0.85 and SD ≈ 11, SEM ≈ 11 × √(1 − 0.85) ≈ **4.3 raw points**. Practical consequence: a student scoring 37 and a student scoring 41 are not reliably different, even though the table places them in different levels. The workbook therefore flags every student within ±4 points of a cut as **"Near Cut"** on the Student Report, and the guidance is always the same — for those students, use the category profile and your own classroom evidence, not the level label.

**Category-level mastery thresholds** (also editable):

| Flag | Category percent | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Mastered** | ≥ 80% | Maintain through spiral review |
| **Approaching** | 60%–79% | Targeted small-group instruction |
| **Not Yet** | < 60% | Explicit reteaching; candidate for Tier 2 |

Category scores rest on as few as 6 points. **A single category percentage is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis.** The Student Report presents category results next to the specific items missed precisely so a teacher confirms the hypothesis against the actual student work before grouping.

## 7. Growth measurement

The reporting workbook computes four growth indicators. They answer different questions, and the fourth is the one to lead with in a data meeting.

1. **Raw gain** = Post − Pre. Simple, but a 5-point gain means something different at 20 than at 50.
2. **Percent-of-possible gain** = (Post − Pre) / (60 − Pre). What share of the ground the student had left to cover did they actually cover? This corrects for ceiling effects on high pre-test scorers.
3. **Reliable Change Index** = (Post − Pre) / (SEM × √2) ≈ (Post − Pre) / 6.1. **A gain of 12 or more raw points is statistically reliable at the individual student level.** Anything smaller may be measurement noise. This is the single most important — and most commonly omitted — number in classroom pre/post reporting, and the workbook prints it for every student.
4. **Class effect size (Cohen's d)** = (Mean_post − Mean_pre) / SD_pooled. Benchmarks for one year of instruction in middle-grades ELA: d ≥ 0.40 is a solid year, d ≥ 0.70 is strong. Reported per section and for the grade level.

**Default individual growth target: +9 raw points** (editable). This sits at roughly half a standard deviation and is deliberately set *below* the reliable-change threshold of 12 so that it functions as an instructional goal rather than a statistical claim. The workbook reports both: "met growth target" and, separately, "reliable change." Do not report a +6 gain as individual student growth. Report it as class-level movement, where the aggregation makes it defensible.

## 8. Administration and fairness conditions

Full detail in `03-Administration-Guide.md`. The technical requirements:

- **Both forms administered under identical conditions.** Same time allocation, same accommodations, same access to sources, same room, same time of day where possible. A pre/post comparison is only valid if the only thing that changed is the instruction.
- **Two sessions of 60 minutes**, or one 120-minute block. Session 1: Parts 1–2 (Interpreting Texts). Session 2: Parts 3–4 (Peer Revision Task and Extended Writing Task). Students may reread Set 2 sources during Session 2 — this is required, not permitted, since Trait 2 depends on source use.
- **Pre-test is not taught to.** If Form A items are reviewed with students, Form B growth is contaminated. Return Form A results, discuss *categories and standards*, and keep the item content secure until after Form B.
- **Accommodations** follow each student's IEP, 504, or EL plan, and must be identical at pre and post. Read-aloud of the Set 2 sources does not invalidate the Constructing Texts score; read-aloud of the Set 1 story does compromise the Interpreting Texts reading measure and should be recorded in the `Accommodation` column so those scores can be interpreted with that in mind.

## 9. Known limitations

Stated plainly, because the alternative is that someone over-reads the data:

- **Cut scores are rationally set, not empirically standard-set.** Level labels are triage categories, not Milestones predictions.
- **Category reliability is low by construction.** Six-point categories cannot support individual high-stakes decisions. Use them to form groups; use student work to confirm them.
- **Single-rater essay scores.** Trait scores from one teacher have a typical exact-agreement rate of 60%–70%. The scoring guide's double-scoring protocol (`rubrics/Writing-Rubric-and-Scoring-Guide.md` §5) raises this materially and takes about 20 minutes per class period.
- **No listening, viewing, speaking, or collaboration measurement.** Standards 7.T.C.1.b (partially), K–12.P.CP.1, and K–12.P.CP.2 cannot be assessed in a paper booklet. They are genuinely part of the Grade 7 standards and must be assessed through classroom performance tasks. This instrument does not cover them and does not pretend to.
- **7.T.PM.1.b is not assessed.** Analyzing a genre of literature from a particular time period requires an extended text study; only 7.T.PM.1.a is measurable here.
- **Field-test items are omitted.** The operational Milestones embeds 8 unscored field-test items. This instrument has none, which slightly shortens testing time relative to the real thing.
