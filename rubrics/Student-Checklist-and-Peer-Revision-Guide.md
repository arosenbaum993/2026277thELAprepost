# Grade 7 Writing Checklist & Peer Revision Guide

*Student-facing. Print double-sided. Use during instruction — not during the pre-test or post-test.*

---

## My Writing Checklist

Check each box only if you can point to the exact place in your draft.

### Trait 1 — Purpose & Organization

- ☐ My **introduction** gives the reader enough background to understand the issue.
- ☐ My **claim** says what I think *and* what should happen. I can underline it.
- ☐ Each body paragraph has **one reason**, and I can say that reason in one sentence.
- ☐ My paragraphs are in an order I chose **on purpose**, not the order I thought of them.
- ☐ My **transitions** name a real relationship (*Beyond that… The stronger objection is… Just as importantly…*), not just *Also* and *Another reason*.
- ☐ My **style** fits my audience. No *basically*, no *stuff*, no *way more*, no talking to my teacher.
- ☐ My **conclusion** finalizes the argument. It does not just list my reasons again.

### Trait 2 — Evidence & Elaboration

- ☐ I used evidence from **BOTH sources**. *(Circle Source 1 evidence in one color and Source 2 evidence in another. If one color is missing, my score is capped.)*
- ☐ Every piece of evidence is attached to a **reason**, not floating on its own.
- ☐ After every piece of evidence, I wrote at least one sentence **in my own words** explaining why it matters.
- ☐ I **credited** my sources by name: *According to Achebe… Boateng reports that…*
- ☐ I stated a **counterclaim** fairly — the way someone who believes it would state it.
- ☐ I **answered** the counterclaim with evidence, not just by saying it is wrong.

### Trait 3 — Language Usage & Conventions

- ☐ Every sentence is complete. No fragments. No run-ons.
- ☐ My sentences are **not all the same length or shape**. I have at least one simple, one compound, and one complex sentence.
- ☐ My **verb tense** stays the same unless I have a reason to change it.
- ☐ My sentences show **who is doing the action** (active voice). I checked for *was/were/is/are/been* + a past-tense verb + *by*.
- ☐ My **modifiers** sit next to the words they describe.
- ☐ I checked commas, semicolons, and apostrophes.

## Peer Revision Guide

You will be given **one trait criterion** to focus on at a time. Do not try to fix everything. Use the three-part questions for the criterion your teacher names.

### How to give feedback that helps

| Instead of… | Say… |
|---|---|
| "This is good." | "Sentence 4 is your claim. I know that because it says what should happen." |
| "I don't get it." | "Between sentence 8 and sentence 9 I lost the connection — what does the turtle fact have to do with cost?" |
| "Add more." | "Reason 2 has evidence but no sentence saying why it matters." |
| "Fix your grammar." | "Sentences 6 and 18 hide who is doing the action." |

**Point to a sentence number every time.** Feedback without a location cannot be used.

---

### TRAIT 1 — Purpose & Organization

**Does the introduction establish a claim?**

1. Does the introduction match the research question? Is the purpose clear?
2. Does the introduction include a clear claim? Is the focus established? Are reasons previewed?
3. *Which sentence should be added at the end of the introduction, or revised, to state the claim and guide the focus?*

**Does the structure group ideas logically?**

1. Read one body paragraph. What reason is presented?
2. Is that reason clearly connected to the claim? Are there any disconnects?
3. *Which sentences should be revised or removed so everything in the paragraph connects to the claim?*

**Is the style right for the purpose and audience?**

1. What is the purpose, and who is the audience?
2. Are any sentences too casual for that audience?
3. *Which TWO sentences should be revised to maintain an appropriate style?*

**Do the transitions create cohesion?**

1. What transitions are used?
2. Do they connect ideas *within* paragraphs and *between* paragraphs?
3. *Which transitions should be revised to best connect the reasons and the evidence?*

**Does the conclusion finalize the response?**

1. What reasons have been explained?
2. Does the conclusion wrap up the main ideas, or just repeat them?
3. *Which sentence should be added to provide the best conclusion — a call to act, think, or decide?*

---

### TRAIT 2 — Evidence & Elaboration

**Do the reasons support the claim?**

1. Does the draft give reasons?
2. Does each reason actually support the claim?
3. *Which reasons need revision to better support the claim?*

**Is the evidence relevant and from multiple sources?**

1. Is there evidence for each reason?
2. Does the evidence come from **more than one source**?
3. *Which piece of evidence from EACH source best connects to the claim?*

**Is there real elaboration?**

1. What facts, details, or examples support each reason?
2. Is there a sentence explaining **why** the evidence supports the reason?
3. *What information should be added to best elaborate on the ideas?*

**Is there a counterclaim?**

1. Does the draft acknowledge an opposing view?
2. Is the counterclaim stated fairly and then answered?
3. *Which sentence should be added to address a counterclaim and strengthen the argument?*

**Are sources credited?**

1. Which sources are used?
2. Is credit given every time information is borrowed — quoted **or** paraphrased?
3. *Which pieces of evidence should be revised to credit the source in the text?*

---

### TRAIT 3 — Language Usage & Conventions

**Sentence structure and variety**

1. What ideas are in each sentence? Which ones belong together?
2. Are any sentences short, choppy, or repetitive? Are conjunctions missing or misused?
3. *Which revision would improve the flow and allow a variety of sentence structures?*

**Verb tense**

1. What tense is used through the paragraph? Does it match the subject?
2. Do any sentences shift tense without a reason?
3. *Which sentences should be revised to correct verb tense?*

**Active voice**

1. Does each sentence clearly show the subject doing the action?
2. Do any sentences sound wordy or unclear?
3. *Which sentences should be revised to maintain active voice?*

**Punctuation**

1. Where does the sentence pause or shift when you read it aloud?
2. Are commas, semicolons, apostrophes, quotation marks, and end punctuation used correctly?
3. *Which sentences contain punctuation errors, and what changes are needed?*

## Quick Reference: Transitions That Name a Relationship

| Relationship | Use |
|---|---|
| Adding a coordinate reason | Just as importantly, · Equally important, · Beyond that, |
| Moving to a new reason | Beyond its effects on ___, · A second reason is that, |
| Giving an example | For example, · For instance, · Consider, |
| Showing cause | Because of this, · As a result, · Consequently, |
| Contrasting | However, · On the other hand, · Even so, |
| Conceding, then answering | Although ___, · It is true that ___, but · Critics argue ___. Yet |
| Concluding | In the end, · The choice comes down to, |

**Warning:** *Also* names no relationship at all. If *Also* is the only transition in your draft, your Trait 1 score is capped at 2.

---

## Quick Reference: Elaboration Sentence Stems

Use one of these after **every** piece of evidence.

- This matters because ___.
- In other words, ___.
- That number means ___.
- What this shows is ___.
- Put another way, if ___, then ___.
- The reason this supports my claim is ___.

---

## Quick Reference: Crediting a Source

- According to **Achebe**, ___.
- **Boateng** reports that ___.
- As **Vanterpool** explains, ___.
- **Whitcomb** points out that ___.
- In "The Vanishing Night," **Achebe** writes that ___.

Credit the source **every time** you borrow — even when you put it in your own words.
